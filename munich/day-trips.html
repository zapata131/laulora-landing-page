⛵ Lake Ammersee & Kloster Andechs

My favorite lake-and-hiking day. Start at the lake in Herrsching, walk up to the "Holy Mountain" for beer, and come back down for sunset by the water.

🚇 How to Get There

Take the S8 toward Herrsching from the lower platforms at Munich Hbf. It's the final stop and takes about 50 minutes.

  • The Hike:

    Follow the signs for "Andechs." It's a 45-60 minute walk through the forest in the Kienbach Valley.

  • Kloster Andechs:

    Go to the Bräustüberl for a liter of Doppelbock and a huge pork knuckle (Haxe). Take a peek inside the Rococo church next door too.

  • The Promenade:

    Herrsching has Germany’s longest lakeside promenade (over 8km), full of ice cream places and cafes like Carlas Kaffeehaus.

  • On the Water:

    Rent a pedal boat, or take the old paddle steamer across the lake to Dießen to see the Marienmünster church.

🏔️ Tegernsee: Beer, Boats & Peaks

Tegernsee is the fancy lake. It's deeper and surrounded by more mountains than Ammersee, so it feels properly Alpine, and it's only an hour away.

🚇 How to Get There

Take the BRB (Bayerische Regiobahn) straight from Munich Hbf. It takes about 1 hour.

💡 Heads up: The train splits halfway, so make sure you're in a carriage marked Tegernsee.

  • Bräustüberl Tegernsee:

    A lot of people say the Helles here is the best in Bavaria. And you drink it right by the water.

  • Wallberg Cable Car:

    Takes you up to 1,722m, where you can see the whole lake and the Austrian mountains.

  • Lakeside Path:

    An easy, flat walk from Tegernsee town to Rottach-Egern. Fancy shop windows on one side, mountains on the other.

🎻 Mittenwald: The Village of 1,000 Violins

Mittenwald sits right under the Karwendel mountains. It's known for its painted houses and for making violins for centuries.

🚇 How to Get There

Take the RB train toward Innsbruck straight from Munich Hbf. It's about 1h 45m, and the Deutschlandticket covers it.

  • Leutasch Spirit Gorge:

    A walkway hanging high above a rushing mountain stream. If you like being outdoors, don't skip the "Ghost Path."

  • Karwendelbahn:

    Germany's second-highest cable car, going up to 2,244m. On a clear day you see Alps in every direction.

  • Lüftlmalerei (Painted Houses):

    Just walk around the center and look up. The old Bavarian houses have these 3D-looking paintings all over the outside walls.

🏰 Neuschwanstein: The Fairytale Castle

The classic one. Pretty much everyone goes, and honestly, it's popular because it really is beautiful.

This is the castle that inspired Disney's. It's probably the most famous building in Germany, sitting on a cliff in the Alps.

🚇 How to Get There

Take the regional train (RE or RB) to Füssen (~2h). From Füssen station, bus 73 or 78 takes you to the ticket center. The Deutschlandticket covers the train and the bus.

  • Marienbrücke:

    The bridge where everyone takes the postcard photo of the castle. It gets crowded, but the photo is worth the wait.

  • The Alpsee:

    A super clear lake right below the castles. Good for a quiet walk when you need a break from the crowds.

  • Booking tickets

    If you want to go inside, book weeks in advance. If they're sold out, go anyway. The outside and the views are worth the trip on their own.

🎶 Salzburg: Mozart & Mountains

Yes, you can go to Austria for the day. Salzburg is a gorgeous Baroque city, known for Mozart, The Sound of Music and a huge fortress on the hill.

🚇 How to Get There

Take the RE 5 train straight to Salzburg Hbf (~1h 45m). And yes, the Deutschlandticket works all the way to Salzburg, even though it's in Austria.

  • Hohensalzburg Fortress:

    One of the biggest medieval castles in Europe that's still fully intact. You can walk up or take the funicular, and the view over the city is great either way.

  • Mirabell Gardens:

    Baroque gardens where they filmed scenes from The Sound of Music. They're free and pretty in any season.

  • Getreidegasse:

    Salzburg's most famous shopping street. Look for house #9, the birthplace of Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart.
