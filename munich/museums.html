🖼️ Munich Museums

Munich has more good museums than you'll have time for, from old paintings to dinosaurs. (And many of them cost only €1 on Sundays.)

⚡ The Sunday €1 Deal

In Munich, Sundays are "Culture Days." Most state-run museums that normally cost €4 to €10 drop to €1.

I'd go to these even at the regular price, but saving money is always nice.

Museum Regular Price What to see Vibe
Alte Pinakothek €9 The "Old Masters" (Da Vinci, Rubens, Dürer). Huge and very grand.
Pinakothek der Moderne €10 4-in-1: Modern Art, Design, Architecture. Lots of daylight.
Museum Brandhorst €7 Pop Art (Warhol) and Cy Twombly. Bright and full of color.
Glyptothek €6 Ancient Greek and Roman statues. Temple vibes & cafe.
Egyptian Museum €7 5,000 years of history underground. Dark, quiet, a bit spooky.
Bavarian National Museum €7 Knight's armor and royal furniture. Bavarian history.
Sammlung Schack €4 Romantic German paintings and myths. Cozy, a little moody.
💡 Sundays get busy

Everyone has the same idea on Sundays. Go right when they open (usually 10:00 AM) or around lunchtime to avoid the crowds.

🚀 Major Highlights

These are the big ones. If you only have time for one or two museums, I'd pick from here.

  • BMW Museum

    A "silver bowl" building all about the history of the car. You don't have to be into cars. I enjoyed the building and seeing how the designs changed over the years. (The free BMW Welt is right next door, more about it on the Essentials page). (~€17)

  • Deutsches Museum

    The world’s largest museum of science and technology. You could spend three days here and not see everything. There are old planes, huge ships, and even mining tunnels. (~€18)

🎨 Quirky Museums

I like these because they're nothing like your usual art gallery.

  • Valentin-Karlstadt-Musäum

    It's inside the Isartor city gate and it's all about Karl Valentin, the "Charlie Chaplin of Bavaria." It’s full of absurd humor and weird inventions like a "winter tooth." (~€4)

  • Beer & Oktoberfest Museum

    It's in the city's oldest townhouse (built in 1340). The floors creak, and you'll understand why beer is basically a food group in Munich. (~€4)

  • MUCA (Street Art)

    Germany’s first street art museum. The murals on the outside are free, and inside there's work by Banksy and Shepard Fairey. (~€15)

🌿 Free (or Almost Free) Museums

Good if you're on a budget. Most of these are free every day.

  • NS-Dokumentationszentrum

    A heavy but very well-made museum on the site of the former Nazi headquarters. It stays with you for a while. (It's very close to the Pinakotheken I mentioned above.)

  • Palais Stuck (Villa Stuck)

    The main exhibitions cost money (~€9), but the historical rooms of this Art Nouveau villa are sometimes open for free or cheaper. For me, the house itself is the best part.

  • MaximiliansForum

    An art space down in a pedestrian underpass. The art is experimental, the place is a bit gritty, and you can walk through any time, 24/7. (Free)

  • Geological & Paleontological Museums

    In the University district (Maxvorstadt). You can just walk in and see dinosaur skeletons and giant crystals. It’s usually very quiet, nice when you need a break from the city. (Free)
