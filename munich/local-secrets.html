✨ Unique Locations

These places feel nothing like the usual "traditional Bavaria" picture. They're a bit rough around the edges and a bit weird, and that's why I like them.

  • Alte Utting (The Ship on a Bridge)

    A 144-ton steamship sitting on top of a railway bridge in Sendling. Yes, really. Inside there's a bar and food market on several levels, with African stews, pizza and a view of the train tracks. It feels more like Brooklyn than Bavaria. It's one of my favorite places for a drink (more places to eat and drink on the Eat & Drink page).

  • Bahnwärter Thiel

    A yard full of old shipping containers, retired subway cars and graffiti. During the day there are artist studios, and at night it turns into techno parties and people hanging out around fires.

  • Gans Woanders

    A little cafe/bar inside a tiny, crooked house that looks like it came out of a fairy tale. It's full of small corners, old furniture, and there's a stage where local bands play. It feels like the neighborhood's secret clubhouse.

  • Werksviertel-Mitte

    This used to be a factory area (they made the potato for Knödel here). Now it's street art, container bars and a giant Ferris wheel, and probably the part of Munich that has changed the most.

  • Hackerbrücke (The Sunset Bridge)

    The big green iron bridge where people hang out on sunny afternoons. Lots of young people sit on the beams to watch the sunset over the train tracks. Get a cheap beer at the Lidl in the ZOB (bus station) right next to it, or bring your own, and you've got a nice afternoon for zero euros.

🌿 Quiet Moments

Munich can be hectic, so these are the places I go when I need a break.

  • "Little Venice" (Untere Weidenstraße)

    A short, quiet bit of the Auermühlbach canal with old workers' cottages along it. It's like a tiny village in the middle of the city.

  • Dichtergarten (Poet’s Garden)

    It's between the Hofgarten and the English Garden, a wilder, more overgrown little park that's often completely empty. It's dedicated to writers, so bring a book and sit for a while.

  • Westpark (Asian Gardens)

    Everyone goes to the English Garden (I talk about it on the Essentials page), but come here too for the Thai Sala and the Nepalese Pagoda. They're right by the lake, and it's a lot calmer.

  • The Isar River (Flaucher Section)

    Rent a bike and ride south along the river until the paved paths turn to pebbles. This is where we go to sunbathe on rocks and listen to the water away from the city noise.

🏛️ Buildings & Design

Things worth seeing that aren't the usual Town Hall or Cathedral.

  • Juristische Bibliothek (Law Library)

    It's inside the Rathaus, but hardly any tourists go in. An Art Nouveau (Jugendstil) library with spiral staircases and gold-trimmed bookshelves. It's a working library, so check the opening hours first.

  • The Infinite Staircase (Umschreibung)

    A sculpture by Olafur Eliasson in an office courtyard (Ganghoferstraße 29). It's a double-helix staircase that goes nowhere. Free to visit, and a fun little surprise.

  • Asamkirche

    A tiny church squeezed between two shops on a busy street. Inside it's gold and skulls everywhere, and I find it more impressive (and weirder) than the giant cathedrals.

  • Müller’sches Volksbad

    One of the most beautiful Art Nouveau swimming pools in the world. If you don't want to swim, you can still peek at the building for free. Or pay a few euros for a "Sauna/Pool" day, which is basically a spa day for cheap.
